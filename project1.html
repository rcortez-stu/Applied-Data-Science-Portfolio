Almond Trees Getting Insomnia

This project investigated whether winter weather conditions could be used to predict almond yield in Fresno County, California. California produces nearly all of the almonds grown in the United States, making yield prediction valuable for growers, insurers, and agricultural planners. The goal was to determine whether winter chill accumulation and temperature patterns were related to annual almond production.

Business Problem

Can measurable winter weather conditions be used as an early indicator of almond yield in Fresno County?

Hypothesis

We want to see if we can make a real connection between the quality or cold pattern of the previous winter and the spring yield of almond crops.

Data Sources

  • NOAA Weather Data
  • USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service (NASS)
  • USDA Census of Agriculture

Tools Used

  • Python
  • Pandas
  • NumPy
  • Matplotlib
  • Seaborn
  • Statsmodels
  • Scikit-learn
  • Jupyter Notebook

Methods

Three public datasets were cleaned and merged into a single analytical dataset. Exploratory data analysis, correlation analysis, simple linear regression, and multiple linear regression were used to evaluate relationships between winter weather variables and almond yield.

Project Visualization

Figure 1. Correlation matrix showing the relationships between winter weather variables and almond yield. This visualization helped identify which weather features were most strongly associated with annual almond production.

Conclusion

The winter climate does affect almond yields. Although not strong enough to make major decisions based on it. It is best used to flag when a season may be trending unusually warm or cold, alongside the many other factors that determine an almond harvest
